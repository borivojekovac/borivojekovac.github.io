# Zeitgeist: Defaults Bite
## May installs AI as a default setting: search that acts, agents that persist, finance in the chat window, sandboxes under the floor. The month is less about raw capability and more about delegation - who gets to decide what the machine does next.

The April address starts billing by the action. In May, the keys move from demos to defaults.

Google opens I/O with [Gemini 3.5 Flash](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-3-5-frontier-intelligence-with-action/ "Google DeepMind on Gemini 3.5"), a fast frontier model sold as action rather than contemplation: Terminal-Bench, GDPval-AA, MCP Atlas, the acronyms clicking like turnstiles. Then the rest of the building lights up. [Antigravity 2.0](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/ "Google on Antigravity 2.0 and the agentic Gemini era") becomes a desktop for orchestrating agents. [Gemini Spark](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/ "Google on Gemini Spark") runs 24/7 on Google Cloud VMs, waiting in the background like hired weather. Search gets [information agents and generative UI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/google-io-2026-collection/ "Google I/O 2026 announcements"), dashboards grown from queries, little interfaces spun from intent.

This is the sharp edge of the month. Search is no longer only a window. It is an actuator. Shopping gets [Universal Cart](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/google-io-2026-collection/ "Google on Universal Cart at I/O 2026"), commerce with an engine under it. Workspace gets [Google Pics](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/ai-design-tools-are-the-next-big-battleground-and-google-is-going-all-in-at-io-2026/ "TechCrunch on Google Pics"), every design object editable by comment, brand polish poured over the prompt box. [Gemini Omni](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-omni/ "Google DeepMind on Gemini Omni") turns image, audio, video, and text into conversational video. Reality gets a cheaper passport.

The street pushes back. DuckDuckGo says installs jump as users reject Google's AI Search, a small consumer mutiny against being [force-fed summaries by default](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/26/duckduckgo-installs-are-up-30-as-users-reject-being-force-fed-googles-ai-search/ "TechCrunch on DuckDuckGo growth after Google AI Search backlash"). Not everyone wants the concierge to answer before the question finishes forming.

OpenAI and Anthropic move through the enterprise side of the same maze. OpenAI spends May turning its April frontier into work surfaces: [Databricks brings GPT-5.5 into enterprise agent workflows](https://openai.com/index/databricks/ "OpenAI on Databricks and GPT-5.5 enterprise agent workflows"), and Codex keeps moving closer to the beige machinery of offices - documents, spreadsheets, data rooms, review queues. Anthropic answers with [Claude Opus 4.8](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-8 "Anthropic on Claude Opus 4.8"), less a fireworks model than a reliability pitch: better judgment, fewer unsupported claims, cleaner tool use. Its [Dynamic Workflows](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-8 "Anthropic on Dynamic Workflows in Claude Code") promise hundreds of parallel subagents, industrial looms weaving migrations, tests, and reports.

Under the interface, the capital stack hums. Anthropic buys air from SpaceX: [300 megawatts, more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs](https://www.anthropic.com/news/higher-limits-spacex "Anthropic on higher Claude limits and SpaceX compute"), immediately translated into higher Claude Code limits. Then it raises [$65 billion at a $965 billion valuation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h "Anthropic on its Series H funding"). Nvidia, supplier and banker, has already committed [more than $40 billion to AI equity deals](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/09/nvidia-has-already-committed-40b-to-equity-ai-deals-this-year/ "TechCrunch on Nvidia AI equity commitments") and discloses [$43 billion in startup holdings](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/nvidia-posts-another-record-quarter-reveals-43-billion-of-holdings-in-startups/ "TechCrunch on Nvidia holdings"). Jensen Huang names a new territory: [Vera, a $200 billion CPU market for agents](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/jensen-huang-says-hes-found-a-brand-new-200b-market-for-nvidia/ "TechCrunch on Nvidia Vera and agent CPUs"). Capacity now has a cap table and a heat plume.

Governance becomes plumbing. OpenAI opens [Trusted Access for Cyber with GPT-5.5-Cyber](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-5-with-trusted-access-for-cyber/ "OpenAI on Trusted Access for Cyber"), permissions wrapped around dual-use capability. Google ships [GKE Agent Sandbox](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/05/gke-agent-sandbox-hypercluster/ "InfoQ on GKE Agent Sandbox and hypercluster") for untrusted agent code. Provenance becomes a surface feature as Google expands [SynthID and C2PA credentials](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/identifying-ai-generated-media-online/ "Google on identifying AI-generated media"). China already has an [AI model registry](https://www.scmp.com/tech/policy/article/3354925/will-chinas-lead-ai-regulation-force-us-rethink-its-approach-under-trump "SCMP on China and US AI regulation"), while Europe argues deadlines and bans.

May's signal is not autonomy. It is delegated default. AI is wired into the places where refusal used to live: search results, office suites, finance chats, codebases, shopping carts, data centers. The issue is no longer raw action. It is whether you noticed who pre-approved it.
