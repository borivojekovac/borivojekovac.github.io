# Google's Agentic Gemini Era Arrives, and It Is Bigger Than Search

Google I/O 2026 was not a normal product refresh. It was Google trying to turn Gemini into the operating layer for search, shopping, software development, office work, consumer assistance, video creation, and eventually eyewear. The company did not just launch a model. It launched an agentic product thesis.

The model foundation was Gemini 3.5 Flash. Google positioned it as the first member of a new Gemini 3.5 family, built around "frontier intelligence with action." The claim was not simply that Flash is smarter. It is that Flash is fast and cheap enough to be used as the default engine for long-horizon work. Google emphasized strong results on agentic and coding benchmarks such as Terminal-Bench, GDPval-AA, and MCP Atlas, and framed Flash as a model that can complete in hours what previously took developers or auditors days.

That speed argument matters because Google wants agents everywhere. Antigravity 2.0 turned what had been an agent-first development platform into a broader desktop environment for managing cohorts of autonomous agents. This is the direction the market has been moving all year: the coding assistant becomes an agent manager, and the agent manager becomes a work surface.

The consumer version of that idea was Gemini Spark, a 24/7 background agent running on Google Cloud virtual machines. Spark is meant to operate without keeping a laptop open, integrate first with Google's own tools, and then connect to third-party systems through MCP. That is a major product boundary shift. Google is no longer saying Gemini can answer questions. It is saying Gemini can keep working.

Search was the most consequential surface. Google introduced information agents that can monitor web, news, social, finance, shopping, and sports data in the background. It also announced generative UI in Search: dynamic layouts, interactive visuals, and eventually persistent dashboards built for an individual query. This turns Search from a retrieval product into a generation and application product. The answer is no longer only a summary; it can become a mini-app.

That ambition collided immediately with user resistance. TechCrunch reported that DuckDuckGo installs jumped after Google's AI Search changes, with users objecting to being force-fed AI summaries and seeking a cleaner AI-free search path. That backlash should not be treated as nostalgia for blue links. It is a signal about control. Users may tolerate AI when they ask for it. They are less comfortable when the search engine changes the default contract.

Google also pushed agentic commerce through Universal Cart. The logic is obvious: if agents can research, compare, monitor prices, and purchase, shopping becomes one of the highest-value consumer workflows. But that also creates new trust problems. Who chooses the seller? Who optimizes for price, delivery, margin, ad spend, or loyalty? An intelligent cart is not neutral infrastructure.

The creative announcements filled out the same platform story. Gemini Omni Flash can generate video from images, audio, video, and text, and edit the result through conversation. Google Pics brought editable AI image generation into Workspace. Google Flow gained more agentic creative planning. Project Genie connected simulated worlds to Street View imagery. Google is trying to collapse the gap between idea, media, interface, and product.

The most important point is that these are not separate announcements. Google is joining four things that used to be distinct: a model family, a developer agent platform, consumer search, and creative generation. Gemini 3.5 Flash gives the cost and latency envelope. Antigravity gives the harness. Search and Workspace give distribution. Omni and Pics give creation.

The open question is whether users want Google to be this agentic by default. May showed Google has the technical and product ambition to make AI the first layer of the web. It also showed that the web's users may demand an off switch.
