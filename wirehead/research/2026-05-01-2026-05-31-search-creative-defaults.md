# The Consumer AI Default Starts to Bite

May was the month consumer AI became less optional. Google put AI deeper into Search, Workspace, shopping, video, and future eyewear. OpenAI moved ChatGPT into personal finance. Anthropic watched Claude move into finance workflows and public debates about human dignity. The pattern was clear: AI is becoming the default interface for sensitive decisions, not just a novelty layer.

Google's I/O announcements were the center of gravity. Search information agents can run in the background across web, news, social, finance, shopping, and sports data. Generative UI in Search will build dynamic layouts and interactive visuals for specific questions. Universal Cart aims to make shopping agentic. Gemini Spark promises persistent background help. Google Pics brings editable image generation into Workspace. Gemini Omni Flash brings conversational video generation and editing into the Gemini app, Google Flow, and YouTube surfaces.

This is an enormous consumer platform bet. It also changes the relationship between user and product. Search used to expose sources. AI Search increasingly interprets, summarizes, acts, and constructs interfaces. Shopping used to be comparison and checkout. Universal Cart implies an agent that can monitor, decide, and possibly transact. Creative tools used to require manipulation of objects. Pics and Omni turn conversation into the editing layer.

The backlash arrived quickly. DuckDuckGo reported app install growth after Google's AI Search changes, and TechCrunch linked it to users rejecting the feeling of being force-fed AI. That reaction matters because it complicates the industry's favorite story about inevitability. People may like AI assistance in narrow contexts while still rejecting AI as the default layer over everything.

The search case is especially sensitive. AI answers can be convenient, but they also centralize interpretation. If the answer is wrong, source traffic declines, attribution gets fuzzy, and the user's ability to compare competing claims weakens. Google can say AI Mode is not the default in every case, but users are reacting to a deeper fear: the open web is being compressed into a managed answer surface.

Creative AI has a different version of the same problem. Gemini Omni is technically impressive because it combines images, audio, video, and text into editable video generation grounded in Gemini's knowledge. But as the generated media becomes easier to create and harder to distinguish, provenance becomes infrastructure. Google's expanded SynthID and C2PA work, plus broader adoption by Nvidia, OpenAI, Kakao, and ElevenLabs, is an admission that generated media needs durable disclosure.

OpenAI's personal finance experience in ChatGPT moved the default problem into an even higher-stakes domain. Finance is not casual productivity. It involves sensitive data, fiduciary expectations, risk tolerance, and the danger that conversational confidence will be mistaken for advice. This is where product design, regulatory boundaries, and model factuality become inseparable.

Google's intelligent eyewear preview points to the next phase. Once AI is in glasses, the assistant is no longer a tab or app. It is ambient perception, memory, and action in physical space. That can be useful. It is also a profound escalation in privacy and attention politics.

May's consumer AI story is not simply that tools got better. It is that defaults changed. AI is moving from "ask when needed" to "present by default, proactive by design, embedded in the path." That shift will generate real utility and real resistance.

The companies that win consumer AI may not be the ones with the strongest models. They may be the ones that give users the clearest control over when AI speaks, when it acts, and when it stays out of the way.
